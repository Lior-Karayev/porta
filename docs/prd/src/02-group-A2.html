4Target Audience and User Types

Primary audience: residents of gated communities in Israel (neighbourhoods, moshavim, kibbutzim, gated residential complexes) whose gate is controlled electronically and who regularly host visitors or service providers. They have a smartphone and messaging apps, and their technical skill ranges from basic to advanced. Secondary audience: the guests (any age, any phone, possibly a first-time visitor) and the community's administrator or committee member.

The product has three roles (feature F-07), plus one special case. A registered guest is a guest who also has an app account. The role table below shows registered guests separately, but they are not a fourth role. For any invitation they receive they have exactly the same rights as an anonymous guest, and the app only changes where they open it.

5Existing Solutions and Differentiation

Below are the categories of solutions in use today, with examples. Statements about specific commercial products are based on general public knowledge and have not been verified hands-on by the team (Q-03). Their feature sets change over time.

Solution / categoryWhat it doesWhat's missing for our audience
Dial-to-open GSM gate controllersThe gate opens when an authorised phone number calls it. Numbers are managed in a list.No day or hour windows and no usage limit per guest. The guest's number must be added and later removed by hand, and there is little or no usable log for residents.
Gate and intercom apps from access-control vendors (e.g. apps bundled with commercial gate controllers)Registered users open gates from a phone app. Some offer guest or temporary access.Usually require the guest to install the app and/or register. Guest options and limits vary, and access tends to be managed by the administrator rather than self-served by the resident.
Smart-lock apps with guest keys (e.g. home smart locks)Time-limited digital keys for a single door.Designed for one home door, not a shared community gate. The guest typically needs the vendor's app, and there is no community administrator layer.
Manual: guard list, phone call, messaging appsThe resident tells the guard or opens remotely. Codes are shared over chat.Depends on people being available. No enforcement of time or usage, no reliable log, and codes leak.

Porta's differentiation

  1. All three limits together: date range, recurring days and hours, and a cumulative opening counter, enforced on the server.
  2. Zero-install guest experience: a single link that works in any modern mobile browser. Registered users can open the same link in the app.
  3. Resident self-service with instant revocation and per-permission visibility. The administrator doesn't become a bottleneck.
  4. AI-assisted creation in the vision: natural-language input and a guided assistant lower the effort of setting up complex recurring access.
