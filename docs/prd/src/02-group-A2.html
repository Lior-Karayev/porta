4Target Audience and User Types

Primary audience: residents of gated communities in Israel (neighbourhoods, moshavim, kibbutzim, gated residential complexes) whose gate is controlled electronically and who regularly host visitors or service providers. They have a smartphone and messaging apps, and their technical skill ranges from basic to advanced. Secondary audience: the guests (any age, any phone, possibly a first-time visitor) and the community's administrator or committee member.

The product has three roles (feature F-07), plus one special case. A registered guest is a guest who also has an app account. The role table below shows registered guests separately, but they are not a fourth role. For any invitation they receive they have exactly the same rights as an anonymous guest, and the app only changes where they open it.

5Existing Solutions and Differentiation

Below are the categories of solutions in use today, with one documented example each. On 26 Nov 2026, the team checked the example products against their official or manufacturer documentation (Appendix B). No product was tested hands-on, so every statement below is "publicly documented", not "verified in use". Remaining hands-on checks are open question Q-03. Feature sets change over time.

Solution / categoryWhat it does (documented example)What's missing for our audience
Dial-to-open GSM gate controllersThe gate opens when an authorised phone number calls it. Numbers are managed in a list. Example: the RTU5024 GSM gate opener. Its user manual documents up to 200 authorised numbers, which can be limited to a specified date and time range.No limit on the number of openings, and no recurring weekly window, were found in the documentation. Numbers are added and removed by whoever manages the device (by SMS command or app), not by each resident. There is no per-resident activity log.
Gate and intercom apps from access-control vendorsRegistered users open gates from a phone app. Example: PalGate (PAL Electronics Systems, Israel). Its App Store listing and support pages say that the site owner or manager adds users (family, guests, tenants) in the app, that access can be time-limited or revoked, and that the gate can also be opened by dialling.The guest is added as an app user, so they need the app. No documented way was found for a resident to send a guest a browser link, or to limit the number of openings.
Smart-lock apps with guest accessDigital access to a single door. Example: Nuki. Its help centre documents users with time restrictions created in Nuki Web, and an invitation that can include an access link, which lets the guest lock and unlock without downloading the Nuki app.Designed for one home door, not a shared community gate. There is no community administrator layer, and no limit on the number of openings was found in the documentation.
Manual: guard list, phone call, messaging appsThe resident tells the guard or opens remotely. Codes are shared over chat.Depends on people being available. No enforcement of time or usage, no reliable log, and codes leak.

Porta's differentiation

  1. All three limits together: date range, recurring days and hours, and a cumulative opening counter, enforced on the server. The documented examples above offer time windows, but none of them documents an opening limit or a recurring weekly window.
  2. Zero-install guest experience: a single link that works in any modern mobile browser. Opening it in the app is optional for later (Could).
  3. Resident self-service for a shared community gate, with instant revocation and per-permission visibility. The administrator doesn't become a bottleneck.
  4. AI-assisted creation: the resident describes the access in a sentence, and Porta drafts the permission for review. This lowers the effort of setting up complex recurring access, and the resident always confirms before anything is saved.
