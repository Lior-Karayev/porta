14Assumptions, Constraints, Dependencies and Risks

14.1 Constraints

  • Time: two weeks from this PRD to the first MVP (12 Dec 2026), and about nine weeks to Porta v1.0 (30 Jan 2027), organised as five 14-day sprints. The period overlaps other courses and the end-of-semester exam season. Nothing finished after 30 Jan counts toward v1.0.
  • Team: three members (Daniel, Lior, Avishag), each with an estimated 8–10 hours per week (to be confirmed). Roles are provisional: Lior is Contributor C; roles A and B are not yet assigned to names (Q-01).
  • Scope: six major feature groups (D-22). Anything else needs a team decision.
  • Knowledge: the team is learning Flutter/Dart during the course (assumed limited prior experience).
  • Budget: zero by default. Any paid service (cloud billing plan, Apple developer account, LLM API, custom domain, AI model credits) needs explicit team approval, with purpose, cost, benefits, free alternatives and the consequences of not buying stated.
  • Hardware: no real gates. Everything is simulated.
  • Course: the app must be developed in Flutter/Dart, follow an Agile process, demonstrate at least five significant technical features, have a Feature PRD per implemented feature, and be delivered according to the course checkpoints (possibly every 7 or 14 days).
  • Process: AI agents follow the shared rules in the repository (AGENTS.md). No agent merges into the main branch: only Lior authorises merges.

14.3 Dependencies (external services the product relies on)

  • A Backend-as-a-Service for authentication, database, server functions and hosting (A-03). Note: Firebase Cloud Functions require the pay-as-you-go (Blaze) plan with a linked billing account, even within free usage quotas. That is a budget decision (Q-09).
  • Push delivery: FCM for Android, and APNs for iOS, which requires an Apple developer account.
  • Domain and HTTPS hosting for invitation links, and for link-verification files if deep links are delivered (Q-15).
  • The Apple Developer Program, for iOS device deployment, Universal Links and push (R-04).
  • Flutter SDK and open-source packages (QR rendering, secure storage, sharing).
  • An LLM API provider for P-F06, only after the provider and cost are approved (Q-04).
  • GitHub (repository, Pull Requests, CI) and the AI development agents (Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity) under their own first-party accounts.

15Milestones and Open Questions

The project follows Agile development in 14-day sprints. A milestone is a delivery date. A sprint is a planning and review cycle. They are tracked separately, and some milestones fall inside a sprint. The instructor may set submission deadlines every 7 or 14 days. Each changed deadline is recorded as a decision, and the plan is updated (R-16). The detailed sprint plan, with tasks, owners, AI support, dependencies, acceptance criteria, integration tests, documentation and risks, is kept in the repository (docs/sprints/SPRINT_PLAN.md).

What it shows: the five 14-day sprints as consecutive blocks, and the milestones on their dates. M1 coincides with the end of Sprint 1. M2 falls inside Sprint 5. Why it matters: the critical access logic is built in Sprints 1–2, leaving two sprints to find bugs. Features are frozen a week before the release. Nothing after 30 January counts toward Porta v1.0. Sprint 5 contains only release verification and blocking fixes before 30 January: no new features (R-01, R-16).
Incomplete sprint work. A sprint ends on its planned date, even if tasks are unfinished. It is never silently extended, and unfinished work is never marked complete. For every incomplete commitment, the team records nine things in a permanent carryover log:
  1. the original task and goal
  2. the work completed
  3. the remaining work
  4. why it was not finished
  5. blockers and dependencies
  6. the impact on future work
  7. the recommended next step
  8. the responsible contributor
  9. the team's decision: carry over, or reduce scope
Earlier sprint outcomes are never erased when the plan changes. Cuts follow the order in Section 7.2.
