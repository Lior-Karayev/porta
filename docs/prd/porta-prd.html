2Product Summary and Vision

What the app does

Porta lets residents of a gated community (a neighbourhood, moshav, kibbutz or gated residential complex) give a guest a defined access permission. The permission says which gates, which dates and hours, and how many gate openings in total.

A permission is either one-time (e.g. dinner guests on Friday evening) or recurring (e.g. a cleaner every Tuesday 08:00–12:00). The resident sends the guest a unique link. The guest opens it in a web browser, with no app install and no registration, and taps "Open gate". The system checks that the permission is valid and then opens the gate.

For the course, all gates are simulated: an opening is shown on a gate-simulator screen. There is no connection to real gate hardware.

Purpose and value to users

  • Residents get control and peace of mind. They grant access without handing out a permanent code or remote, suspend or cancel it with one tap, and see who entered and when.
  • Guests and service providers get in without phoning the resident or waiting at the gate, and without installing an app.
  • Community administrators get an audit trail of every gate access and no forgotten "permanent" permissions.

Vision: the full product

Porta aims to become the community's access-management layer:

  • Residents manage contacts, templates and group invitations.
  • Administrators get reports, alerts on unusual activity, and an approval workflow for sensitive permissions.
  • Creating a permission becomes as easy as writing a sentence ("let the gardener in every Sunday morning until the end of the year"), through AI-assisted natural-language creation and a guided assistant.

Real gate controllers may be connected in the future. That integration is explicitly out of scope for this course (Section 7.4).

3Problem and Need

Who has the problem, and what exactly is it

WhoDifficulty
Residents hosting visitorsWhen a visitor arrives, the resident has to be available at that exact moment to answer the phone, walk to the gate or ask the guard. The alternative is to hand over a permanent code, remote or dial-in number, and then they cannot control when or how often it is used.
Residents with regular service providersA weekly service provider usually gets permanent access with no day or hour limits. When the engagement ends, revoking that access is easily forgotten.
Guests and service providersThey wait at the gate, call repeatedly, or are stuck when the resident is unreachable.
Community administrator / committeeThey have no overview of who was given access, until when, and who actually entered. Forgotten access accumulates.

How it is handled today, and what doesn't work

  • Dial-to-open (GSM) phone-number lists: the guest's number is added to a list, typically with no expiry. Revoking it depends on someone remembering to remove it.
  • Shared permanent codes or remotes: codes leak and get passed on with no control. Revoking one means changing the code for every resident.
  • Remote opening by the resident or a guard: requires the resident to be available at the moment of arrival. This fails in meetings, while driving, or on Shabbat.
  • A manual list kept by a guard: does not handle recurring day and hour access, has no reliable record, and depends on staffing.

Why the proposed solution is needed, and evidence the problem is real

The need is for access that is limited by time window, by days and hours, and by number of uses together; revocable instantly; and logged, without the host being present for every entry. Each current method covers only part of this.

Transparency about evidence: no user research has been carried out yet. The problem statement comes from the team members' own experience and general familiarity with the access methods above, not from collected data. A short validation (Q-02) is planned before the MVP is complete: interviews or a questionnaire with at least 5 residents of gated communities. Its findings will be added to this document.

Porta's differentiation

  1. All three limits together: date range, recurring days and hours, and a cumulative opening counter, enforced on the server.
  2. Zero-install guest experience: a single link that works in any modern mobile browser. Registered users can open the same link in the app.
  3. Resident self-service with instant revocation and per-permission visibility. The administrator doesn't become a bottleneck.
  4. AI-assisted creation in the vision: natural-language input and a guided assistant lower the effort of setting up complex recurring access.

7.3 Developed later, after the MVP

  • M5 · Guest management: group invitations (F-12). Templates (F-05) and contact directory (F-11), if they were not delivered as stretch goals.
  • M6 · Community governance: administrator reports and analytics (F-16). Unusual-activity detection (F-17). Administrator approval for sensitive permissions (F-18).
  • M7 · AI: natural-language creation (F-19), if it was not delivered as a stretch goal. Smart reminders and suggestions (F-20). An AI-enhanced guided assistant (F-21).

7.4 Explicitly out of scope for this project

  • Integration with real gate hardware or controllers. All gates are simulated.
  • Residents' own entry and exit through Porta, and vehicle or license-plate recognition.
  • Video intercom, voice calls, and guard-station applications.
  • Payments, billing, and multi-community SaaS administration. One user belongs to one community in the MVP.
  • Self-service community creation and transferring the administrator role. Communities and their first administrator are provisioned by the team (A-11).
  • A separate desktop web admin portal. Administrator functions are in the mobile app in the MVP.
  • Opening a gate offline (deliberately prohibited, see NFR-14). Proximity or geofence verification (possible future work).
  • Legal compliance certification. Privacy principles are applied (Section 13), but no formal compliance audit is planned.

7.5 AI feasibility decision for the MVP

Proposed decision, pending team approval (Q-04): the MVP does not depend on any AI capability.

  1. F-21 Guided assistant enters the MVP as a deterministic, rule-based step-by-step wizard (Should). It delivers the guided experience with no external dependency, cost or unpredictability.
  2. F-19 Natural-language creation is a Could stretch goal. It is started only after every Must and Should item passes, and only if the team approves the LLM API cost (Budget Policy). Its design is safe by construction: the AI only drafts fields, the resident must confirm, and the system falls back to the manual form (FR-35, US-13).
  3. F-20 Smart reminders and suggestions is scheduled for M7. It needs usage history that the MVP will not have.

Why: about 10 weeks remain until 31 Jan 2027 and the team is new to Flutter. An external LLM adds paid usage, latency, non-deterministic output and a new failure mode (R-06). Putting the core access logic at risk for an optional feature would go against the project's first priority.

Course alignment: the team should confirm whether the course requires an AI feature inside the MVP (Q-04). If it does, F-19 moves from Could to Should, and F-15 and F-10 move down to Could to free up capacity.

What it shows: the server-side handling of every opening request. The table lists the checks in order, each with its denial reason. Why it matters:
  • All reads and writes happen inside one atomic transaction: the receipt, invitation, permission and gate checks, and then the counter, gate open-until time, event and receipt writes. 11.4 The AI component (FR-35, Could)

InputThe resident's free text (≤ 300 characters), today's date, the community time zone, and the names of the community's active gates. No personal data beyond the guest name the resident typed. No phone numbers, no history.
OutputA structured draft: type, validFrom, validUntil, daysOfWeek, startTime, endTime, gateNames, maxOpenings, guestName, plus a list of fields it could not determine. The output is checked against a strict schema.
When it is wrongThe draft is always shown in the normal form for review, and nothing is saved without the resident's confirmation. Values outside FR-06/FR-07 limits, or gate names that don't match a real gate, are cleared and flagged. The server re-validates every field exactly as for manual input.
When it is unavailableTimeout after 8 s, or any error: the manual form opens with the text preserved (AC-13.2). Core functionality never depends on the AI.
Cost and approvalRequires a paid API key. It needs explicit team approval of cost before any use (Budget Policy, Q-04).
